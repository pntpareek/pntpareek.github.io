Statement of Past Research

  • Various time scales in passive glassy dynamics: We found that caging controls the mean, whereas dynamical heterogeneity (DH) governs the distribution of particle displacements. In addition, we can define several other time scales, such as when the mean-square displacement becomes diffusive, the distribution of particle displacement becomes Gaussian, the overlap function becomes a power-law, the intermediate scattering function becomes exponential, etc. We demonstrated using analytical calculations and simulation results that all these different timescales are related to either caging or DH. This naturally explains the Fickian yet non-Gaussian behaviour of glasses.
  • Crossover in autocorrelation function from stretched exponential to power-law: The overlap function in passive glasses shows a crossover from stretched exponential (SE) at short times to a power law at long times. To understand the mechanism behind such a crossover, we defined a simplified one-dimensional spin model that belongs to the class of kinetically constrained models. The model has an onsite energy function and a kinetic rule that a spin can flip only if the neighbouring spins have opposite signs. Thus, the dynamics conserve energy, which comes from the movements of domain walls (DWs). We found that the crossover comes from a change in the relevant excitations: from DWs at short times to bound pairs of DWs at long times.
  • Influence of activity on relaxation dynamics and fragility in active glasses: Active glasses comprise self-propelled particles with a self-propulsion force f0 and persistence time τp and provide a minimal model for biological systems. They often show nontrivial behaviors, such as reentrant dynamics and tunable fragility. We studied a dense active system of soft repulsive particles that shows these features. We characterised the critical surface separating liquid and glassy states and showed that the nontrivial features are consequences of the morphology of this surface. In addition, with increasing τp, the nature of the critical point changes from glass-like to jamming-like. The tunable fragility, where the dynamics goes from sub-Arrhenius to super-Arrhenius, also appears in biological tissues. We employed a dynamic scaling analysis and showed that this behavior in the current model is governed by an “active glass line”.
